Fresh Banana Competitive Intelligence

Walmart compared directly with ALDI and Amazon using the complete August 7, 2026 “bananas” collections.

Primary scope: fresh edible bananas and plantains sold as produce. Dried/freeze-dried bananas, chips, frozen sliced bananas, snacks, books, accessories and other search noise are excluded from the primary competitive analysis.

Executive Summary

  • The full datasets were analyzed. All 168,440 source rows were processed—96,994 Walmart, 4,429 ALDI and 67,017 Amazon—with no store or ZIP subsampling.
  • Walmart has the broadest observed fresh-banana footprint. Qualifying fresh bananas/plantains surfaced in 3,867 Walmart ZIPs / 4,296 stores. ALDI covered 2,418 ZIPs / 2,537 stores. Amazon had available qualifying fresh produce in 1,450 ZIPs.
  • ALDI’s $0.16 banana looks much cheaper per piece, but the weight-normalized gap is minimal. In 1,464 exact overlapping ZIPs, ALDI’s conventional banana was $0.16 each versus Walmart at $0.20 each—a 20% piece-price advantage. Retailer unit metadata puts those same items at approximately $0.49/lb ALDI versus $0.50/lb Walmart, only about a 2% weight-normalized difference.
  • Amazon standard banana pricing is highly market-dependent. Across 485 exact each-to-each ZIP matches, Walmart was lower in 56.3% and Amazon in 43.7%; the median Amazon premium was only +$0.01 per banana.
  • Organic bunch package price is a misleading headline. Amazon’s common 4-5 count organic bunch was $1.49 versus Walmart’s $1.74 bunch, but Walmart’s bunch contains approximately 5-7 bananas. Using midpoint counts, Walmart’s estimated price per banana was lower in 95.8% of 741 comparable ZIPs.

Amazon 4-5 count conventional bunch

Amazon’s common bunch price was $0.99 in 811 Walmart-overlap ZIPs. A 4-5 count bunch implies approximately:

  • $0.198 per banana if the bunch contains 5 bananas
  • $0.248 per banana if it contains 4 bananas
  • Walmart standard banana: $0.20 each
Interpretation: the $0.99 package does not establish an Amazon unit-price advantage. At five bananas it is essentially parity; at four bananas Walmart is materially lower.

Recommended Actions for Walmart Price Intelligence

  1. Track both each price and weight-normalized price for conventional bananas. ALDI’s $0.16 each creates a strong shelf-price perception versus Walmart’s $0.20, even though the underlying $/lb difference is only about one cent.
  2. Keep plantains separate from bananas. Walmart holds a clear each-price advantage versus ALDI and the limited Amazon overlaps.
  3. Create an Amazon standard-banana ZIP watchlist. Amazon wins 43.7% of exact each-price matches, but the price varies widely by local market from $0.12 to $0.45 each.
  4. Normalize bunches by count before escalating price gaps. Amazon’s $0.99 conventional bunch and $1.49 organic bunch can appear lower simply because the package contains fewer bananas.
  5. Use weight-normalized organic comparisons whenever weight is available. Walmart’s documented organic economics are approximately $0.74/lb; fixed-weight Amazon organic offers in the matched sample were materially higher.
  6. Keep variety/type in the comparison key. Standard yellow, green/cooking, plantain, manzano/apple, burro, red and mini/baby bananas should not be blended.
  7. Repeat the collection over time. Produce price and average piece weight can change by market, season and supplier; recurring collection is needed before treating a one-day price architecture as permanent.

Methodology and Caveats

  • Full coverage: every row in all three supplied files was processed—168,440 records total—with no store or ZIP sampling.
  • Fresh-produce scope: fresh bananas and plantains were retained. Dried/freeze-dried, frozen, chips/snacks, powders, nonfood items and unrelated fruit search results were excluded from primary price analysis.
  • Amazon availability: Amazon footprint excludes records explicitly marked out of stock. Zero-price records remain eligible for presence only when availability is not explicitly false, but zero prices are never used in price comparisons.
  • Exact matching: same ZIP, banana/plantain variety, organic status and selling unit are required. Weight- or count-normalized comparisons are clearly labeled separately.
  • Brand: explicit brand is retained. Because much fresh banana produce is unbranded or “brands vary,” brand-neutral spec equivalence is the primary competitive lens; no material same-brand national comparison exists across the three files.
  • Retailer unit metadata: official Walmart and ALDI product pages were used to interpret the selling-unit semantics that are not fully represented in the CSV. Walmart’s standard banana is approximately $0.50/lb at a $0.20 estimated each price; ALDI’s is approximately $0.49/lb at a $0.16 estimated each price. Walmart states its organic bunch contains approximately 5-7 bananas and lists approximately $0.74/lb.
  • Amazon same-day: qualifying available fresh produce appeared in 1,450 ZIPs, but explicit “Today” or “Overnight” delivery text appeared on 100 rows across 62 ZIPs. Search-result presence is broader than item-level same-day confirmation.
  • Count-range comparisons: midpoint per-banana estimates use 4.5 bananas for a 4-5 count Amazon bunch and 6 bananas for Walmart’s documented 5-7 count organic bunch. These are analytical estimates, not guaranteed piece counts.
