What Walmart Leadership Should Do

  1. Treat ALDI as the primary value-price watchlist. Its broad 2,472-ZIP footprint and majority win rate in strict comparisons make it the strongest price-pressure signal in this collection.
  2. Maintain an Amazon urban and rapid-delivery watchlist. Amazon’s overall shell-egg footprint is narrower, but its 1,380 covered ZIPs and item-level same-day language in 53 ZIPs justify targeted monitoring.
  3. Use regional retailer playbooks. Safeway and Albertsons show large observed premiums versus Walmart; H-E-B, Meijer, Target, ShopRite, Giant Eagle, Wegmans, and Kroger should be monitored within their operating geographies rather than through a single national average.
  4. Keep count and production claims in the comparison key. Large price differences can reverse when the analysis moves between conventional, cage-free, free-range, pasture-raised, organic, and club-size packs.
  5. Improve price-data completeness before automating alerts. Positive prices were captured on only 53.9% of Target and 73.5% of Safeway qualifying rows; Kroger’s package count was identifiable on 26.8% of qualifying rows.

Methodology and Required Caveats

  • Full coverage: every row and every location in the supplied file was processed. “Collected ZIPs” refers to the complete location universe present for that retailer in the file, not a smaller analytical sample.
  • Product classification: title and URL metadata were used to identify raw fresh chicken shell eggs and extract count, size, color, grade, organic status, and housing claim. Obvious search noise and prepared/non-shell products were excluded.
  • Deduplication: repeated search results were deduplicated at retailer, ZIP, store, product ID, and title. All stores remained represented.
  • Price comparison: comparisons require the same ZIP. The strict scorecard requires all requested product attributes to align; the compatible-spec scorecard only relaxes color or grade when one side is unknown.
  • Price availability: only positive captured prices were used in price comparisons. Search-result presence still contributes to geographic coverage when price parsing was blank or zero.
  • Coverage interpretation: retailer collection footprints differ. ZIP and state counts describe the supplied retailer-specific location coverage and should not be treated as national market share.
  • Amazon delivery: Amazon search-result presence is broader than explicit item-level same-day confirmation. Only rows containing “Today” or “Overnight” were counted as explicitly confirmed rapid-delivery text.
