Walmart Leadership · Price Intelligence

Milk Competitive Intelligence

Walmart compared directly with ALDI and Amazon across the complete supplied “milk” collections.

Primary scope: fresh/refrigerated, single-container cow’s milk. Comparisons preserve container size, fat level/type, flavor, organic status, lactose-free/ultrafiltered and other specialty claims, with brand handled explicitly.

Executive Summary

  • The full datasets were analyzed. All 348,980 source rows were processed: 240,779 Walmart, 41,321 ALDI and 66,880 Amazon records. No store or ZIP sampling was used.
  • Walmart has the broadest store-based footprint. Fresh fluid dairy milk surfaced in all 4,146 Walmart ZIPs and 4,596 Walmart stores in the collection. ALDI covered 2,472 ZIPs / 2,595 stores; Amazon surfaced qualifying fresh milk in 3,411 of 4,166 collected ZIPs.
  • Walmart leads ALDI on conventional milk, but ALDI is sharper in several premium/specialty segments. In private-label equivalent matches, Walmart was lower in 59.6% and ALDI in 40.4%. Walmart won essentially all conventional gallon and half-gallon whole/2% matches, while ALDI led organic and lactose-free half-gallon segments.
  • Amazon is a mixed story depending on brand treatment. On the exact same national brands, 35.4% of matches were at price parity and the median gap was $0.00/gallon. Amazon-owned/private-label milk was lower in 52.9% of matched observations, while Walmart was lower in 45.4%.

How Milk Is Matched

Same-brand exact
Same ZIP + same brand + same size, fat/type, flavor, organic, lactose-free, ultrafiltered, A2, Omega-3/DHA, grass-fed, kids and protein-fortified flags.
Private-label equivalent
Walmart-owned labels vs retailer-owned labels with the same product specifications. This is the primary ALDI value comparison.
All-brand equivalent
Same ZIP and product specifications regardless of brand, using each retailer’s lowest qualifying shelf price.

All price gaps are normalized to a gallon equivalent, but products must still match on actual container size; a gallon is not matched directly to a half gallon.

ALDI: Walmart Wins Conventional Milk; ALDI Wins Several Specialty Segments

Private-label equivalent result

16,353 matched observations across 1,568 ZIPs.

  • Walmart lower: 59.6%
  • ALDI lower: 40.4%
  • Median ALDI-minus-Walmart gap: +$0.76 per gallon
Conventional milk favors Walmart. Walmart was lower in essentially 100% of matched gallon whole, gallon 2%, half-gallon whole, half-gallon 2%, half-gallon 1% and half-gallon skim private-label observations.

Where ALDI applies pressure

  • Half-gallon organic whole: ALDI lower in 100% of 1,358 private-label matches; median advantage $0.24/gallon.
  • Half-gallon organic 2%: ALDI lower in 98.7% of 1,335 matches; median advantage $0.24/gallon.
  • Half-gallon lactose-free whole: ALDI lower in 100% of 1,436 matches; median advantage $0.18/gallon.
  • Half-gallon lactose-free 2%: ALDI lower in 100% of 1,403 matches; median advantage $0.18/gallon.
  • Gallon 1%: ALDI lower in 100% of 1,047 private-label matches; median advantage $0.37/gallon.

Amazon: Same-Brand Pricing Is Often at Parity, While Private Label Is More Aggressive

Exact same-brand comparison

7,917 matches across 1,720 ZIPs.

  • Walmart lower: 37.2%
  • Amazon lower: 27.4%
  • Parity: 35.4%
  • Median price gap: $0.00 per gallon

This is the cleanest national-brand benchmark because brand and product attributes both align.

Amazon-owned/private-label comparison

7,402 matches across 1,657 ZIPs.

  • Walmart lower: 45.4%
  • Amazon lower: 52.9%
  • Median Amazon-minus-Walmart gap: $-0.18 per gallon
Amazon private label is the bigger value threat. Amazon was lower in a slight majority of matched private-label observations, especially gallon 1%, gallon skim/fat-free and half-gallon 2%.

Recommended Actions for Walmart Price Intelligence

  1. Protect Walmart’s conventional milk price advantage against ALDI. Gallon and half-gallon whole/2% are consistently favorable to Walmart in the observed overlapping ZIPs.
  2. Create specialty-milk alerts for ALDI. Organic and lactose-free half-gallon milk—and gallon 1%—are the clearest ALDI price-pressure areas.
  3. Split Amazon monitoring into national-brand and private-label scorecards. Same-brand Amazon pricing is frequently at parity; Amazon-owned/Whole Foods private label is more aggressive in several core milk types.
  4. Watch Amazon Organic Valley separately. Amazon was lower in 57.9% of same-brand Organic Valley matches, with a median advantage of about $0.54/gallon.
  5. Preserve size and specialty attributes in automated price alerts. Do not compare gallon vs. half gallon or standard milk vs. organic/lactose-free/ultrafiltered/A2 products simply because all are returned by “milk.”
  6. Repeat the collection over time. Milk is highly local and regional; recurring runs will distinguish stable price architecture from promotions, local dairy economics and temporary availability.

Methodology and Caveats

  • Full coverage: all 348,980 source rows and every supplied Walmart/ALDI store and Amazon ZIP were processed.
  • Primary category scope: fresh/refrigerated, single-container cow’s milk. Plant-based beverages, powders/formula, evaporated/condensed milk, creamers/cream, buttermilk, shelf-stable multipacks and obvious search noise were excluded from primary pricing comparisons.
  • Attribute extraction: product titles were used first; URLs were used only as a fallback when the title omitted container size. This was especially important for ALDI.
  • Brand treatment: same-brand exact matches require the identical normalized brand. Private-label comparisons are limited to retailer-owned labels; all-brand equivalent matches intentionally ignore brand but preserve all product specifications.
  • Price normalization: gap values are shown on a gallon-equivalent basis for easier interpretation, but products still must match on actual package volume.
  • Amazon fulfillment: Amazon fresh milk surfaced in 3,411 ZIPs, but explicit “Today” or “Overnight” delivery language appeared on 1,408 qualifying rows across 174 ZIPs. Search-result presence is stronger evidence than checkout-level same-day confirmation.
  • Price completeness: Walmart and ALDI qualifying rows had effectively complete positive price capture; Amazon price capture was 90.1%.
  • Geography: ZIP-to-state enrichment uses the public ReadyAPIs curated U.S. ZIP dataset, supplemented by latitude/longitude already present in the supplied retailer files.
